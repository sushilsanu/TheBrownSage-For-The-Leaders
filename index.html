What we are building

A navigation intelligence layer for oncology.

An Indian cancer patient's journey begins not with a clinical conversation, but with a stack of mismatched records — biopsies from one lab, imaging from another, OPD notes from a third hospital, prescriptions from a fourth. Before any specialist can think clearly about the case, those records have to be read, structured, and understood. That hour of structuring work — multiplied across every consultation, every second opinion, every tumour board — is where most of Indian oncology's lost time lives.

The Brown Sage is a platform that does the structuring before the clinical conversation begins. It reads a patient's records, organises them into a structured clinical view, and routes that case — by clinical fit — to a senior oncologist, to a tumour board, to a clinical trial match, to a regional referral network.

In plain words: it is a navigation intelligence layer for oncology. The work is not summarisation. It is orientation — knowing where a case actually stands, and who it should reach.

The architecture, briefly

Three layers, working in sequence.

The platform is built as three distinct layers of intelligence. Each does one thing well; the work compounds across them.

L1 Clinical Intelligence
Reads and structures the patient's records — every report linked to its source, every value tagged with confidence and extraction status, every conflict between documents flagged for human resolution. The case that reaches the specialist is not a folder of mixed documents; it is a verified clinical view.
L2 Matchmaking Intelligence
Routes a structured case to the clinical mind best suited to read it — by sub-specialty, by case-type focus, by clinical depth. This is the layer that does the real translation work: putting the right case in front of the right reader, wherever the case ultimately goes — to a written response, a tumour board, a referral, a trial.
L3 Knowledge Layer
Scores every case against published guideline standards — National Cancer Grid (India's Tata Memorial-led standard), ICMR, and NCCN. Output is FHIR-interoperable; any hospital EMR or tumour board system can consume it directly. The patient sees how their plan compares to what India's top cancer centres recommend for their exact profile.

L1 reads. L2 routes. L3 knows. The compounding across these three layers is where the platform's real value lives.

Two questions we expect

The shape of two reasonable concerns.

Senior leaders in oncology see a great deal of health-tech in motion. Two questions tend to come up early; we have thought about them carefully and answer them here in the same register as the rest of this page.

How is The Brown Sage Clinical Intelligence different from a general AI tool?

A patient or clinician can upload records to a general AI tool and ask it questions; many already do. The structural difference is in what arrives on the desk, and what has been done to it before it gets there.

Source-linked, not chat-generated. Every extracted field carries its source quote and source document. Every field is tagged with confidence (high / medium / low) and extraction status (confirmed / inferred). A general AI tool produces confident text regardless of whether the underlying signal is solid; this system surfaces the signal-strength of every value explicitly.

Conflict-resolved, not silently blended. Case fusion across multiple documents applies explicit source priority by document type, then recency, then confidence. Conflicts that cannot be safely auto-resolved are surfaced for human adjudication and blocked from driving guideline selection until reviewed.

Verified by a Case Manager, not the patient. A Case Manager reviews every extracted value against the source records before the case reaches a clinical reader. Completeness is a precondition, not an afterthought — cancer-specific playbooks flag missing required documents before the case is routed.

The AI here works for the clinician, not in place of the clinician. The clinical reasoning that follows is entirely a human's.

How is data privacy handled?

Patient records are protected by three architectural commitments, each built into the platform rather than added on.

Seen only by the case team, not by anyone else. Records are encrypted at rest and in transit. Access is limited to the Case Manager who structures the case and the clinician who reads it — and each only for the workflow window that requires it. No marketing access, no engineering access, no general staff access. Full compliance with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.

Used only for the case, not for anything else. The identifiable records uploaded by a patient are used only for that patient's case. They are never used to train our models, never sold, never shared. Research on properly de-identified data is something the platform will support in the future, with patient consent — but no such use exists today.

Owned by the patient, not by the platform. Patients can request deletion of their records at any time, honoured within 7 working days. Records also remain downloadable to the patient for as long as they want them. The platform is a stop on the journey, not a destination.

A note on the work

The work has just begun.

The platform is live, and the first cases are beginning to move through it. The architecture is in place. But this is the earliest part of a long journey. The depth of clinical learning ahead — across cancer types, treatment intents, decision points, edge cases — is exactly the work that lies in front of us, not behind.

The platform will get sharper with every case it sees. The structured extraction will get more reliable. The matchmaking will get more precise. The guideline-concordance scoring will deepen. None of this is finished, and none of it pretends to be.

This is precisely why we are reaching out to you, not later but now. The perspectives that shape this work in its early form will shape what it becomes.
